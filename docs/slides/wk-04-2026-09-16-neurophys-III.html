Warm-up

What initiates the action potential?

  • A. Rapid influx of K+ ions that makes the membrane potential more negative.
  • B. Rapid outflow of A- ions that makes the membrane potential more negative.
  • C. Input depolarizes (makes positive) the cell to the threshold voltage (~55 mv).

Answer

  • A. Rapid influx of K+ ions that makes the membrane potential more negative.
  • B. Rapid outflow of A- ions that makes the membrane potential more negative.
  • C. Input depolarizes (makes positive) the cell to the threshold voltage (~55 mv).

The rising (depolarization) phase of the action potential occurs due to

  • A. Opening of voltage-gated Na+ channels and Na+ inflow
  • B. Opening of voltage-gated K+ channels and K+ outflow
  • C. Closing of voltage-gated Na+ channels that stops rapid Na+ inflow

Answer

  • A. Opening of voltage-gated Na+ channels and rapid Na+ inflow
  • B. Opening of voltage-gated K+ channels and rapid K+ outflow
  • C. Closing of voltage-gated Na+ channels that stops rapid Na+ inflow

The peak of the action potential occurs due to

  • A. Opening of voltage-gated Na+ channels and Na+ inflow
  • B. Opening of voltage-gated K+ channels and K+ outflow
  • C. Closing of voltage-gated Na+ channels that stops rapid Na+ inflow

Answer

  • A. Opening of voltage-gated Na+ channels and rapid Na+ inflow
  • B. Opening of voltage-gated K+ channels and rapid K+ outflow
  • C. Closing of voltage-gated Na+ channels that stops rapid Na+ inflow

The falling (repolarization) phase of the action potential occurs due to

  • A. Opening of voltage-gated Na+ channels and Na+ inflow
  • B. Opening of voltage-gated K+ channels and K+ outflow
  • C. Closing of voltage-gated Na+ channels that stops rapid Na+ inflow

Answer

  • A. Opening of voltage-gated Na+ channels and rapid Na+ inflow
  • B. Opening of voltage-gated K+ channels and rapid K+ outflow
  • C. Closing of voltage-gated Na+ channels that stops rapid Na+ inflow

Key facts

  • Na/K pump creates concentration gradients
    • [Na+] outside >> inside \(\rightarrow\) Na+ flows in
    • [K+] inside >> outside \(\rightarrow\) K+ flows out
  • Ion channels accelerate/slow ion flow
    • Open ion channel: Accelerate flow
    • Close ion channel: Slow flow

Key facts

  • Membrane potential magnitude & sign accelerates/slows ion flow
    • More - \(\rightarrow\) attracts + charges
    • More + \(\rightarrow\) repels + charges
  • Ion flow changes membrane potential
    • Na+ inflow \(\rightarrow\) membrane potential (voltage) more +
    • K+ outflow \(\rightarrow\) membrane potential (voltage) more -

Why should I care?

  • Action potentials are the language of thought
  • Practice decomposing complex systems with interacting elements
  • Mastery leads to predictive power

Neurophysiology III

Propagation

  • Unmyelinated axons
    • Each segment “excites” the next

Propagation

  • Myelinated axon
    • AP “jumps” between Nodes of Ranvier
    • voltage-gated \(Na^+\), \(K^+\) channels exposed
    • current flows through/down myelinated segments

Nodes of Ranvier

  • Regenerate action potential
  • \(Na^+\) and \(K^+\) channels exposed to extracellular environment
  • Between Nodes of Ranvier, ions can’t move out, so move along

Nodes of Ranvier

  • Action potentials faster & more reliable for a given axon diameter

Why does AP flow in one direction, away from soma?

  • Soma does not have (many) voltage-gated Na+ channels.
  • Soma is not myelinated.
  • Refractory periods mean polarization works only in one direction.

Why does AP flow in one direction, away from soma?

  • Soma does not have (many) voltage-gated Na+ channels.
  • Soma is not myelinated.
  • Refractory periods mean polarization works only in one direction.

How fast are APs?

  • Axons carry information at different rates
    • Ticker myelin -> faster
    • Larger diameter axon -> faster
  • Somatosensory information faster than pain (nociception)

Information processing

  • Action potential amplitudes don’t vary (much)
    • All or none (fire or don’t fire)
    • \(Na^+\)/\(K^+\) pumps working all the time
    • \([K^+]\) & \([Na^+]\) don’t vary much, so
    • \(V_{K^+}\) & \(V_{Na^+}\) don’t vary much

Information processing

  • Action potential frequency and timing vary
    • Rate vs. timing codes
    • Neurons use both
  • Action potentials analogous to 1s and 0s used as bits of information in digital computers

Anatomy: Spinal cord & PNS

Spinal cord

  • Rostral/Caudal axis
    • Spinal column w/ vertebrae
    • Cervical (8), thoracic (12), lumbar (5), sacral (5), coccygeal (1)
    • Spinal segments & 31 nerve pairs
    • Cauda equina

Spinal cord

  • Nerves map to specific regions of the body
  • Dermatomes

Spinal cord

  • Dorsal/Ventral
    • Dorsal root (sensory)
    • Ventral root (mostly motor)
  • Grey (interior) vs. white matter (exterior)

Spinal cord

  • Afferent (input/sensory) and efferent (output/motor) tracts

Organization of the PNS

  • Somatic division
    • Voluntary sensory & motor function
  • Autonomic division
    • Involuntary sensory & motor function
  • Cranial nerves
  • Spinal nerves

Cranial nerves

  • Sensory & motor functions of the cranium (head) and neck
  • Afferents (input/sensory), efferents (output/motor), or mixed/both
    • On Old Olympus’ Towering Top…
    • Some Say Marry Money…

Cranial nerves

  • Innervate head and neck
  • Olfactory (I), optic (II), (VIII) auditory, vagus (X), etc.

Autonomic nervous system (ANS)

  • CNS & PNS components
  • Controls “vegetative functions””
    • Limited voluntary control
  • Two divisions
    • Sympathetic
    • Parasympathetic

Sympathetic division

  • Prepares body for action
  • “Fight or flight”
  • Spinal cord
    • ganglion chain along spinal column to end organs

Sympathetic division

  • Neurotransmitters
    • Preganglionic: Acetylcholine (ACh)
    • Post: Norepinephrine (NE)

Parasympathetic division

  • Para -> “around”
  • Restorative function
  • “Rest & digest”
  • Spinal cord -> ganglia near end organs -> end organ
    • Neurotransmitter: Acetylcholine (ACh)
